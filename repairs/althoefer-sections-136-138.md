## 136. The typed marked/obligation normalizer is well-founded in each retained fibre

The previous version of this section used the notation
\(\sigma=\omega^\omega a+\Psi(N)\) without keeping a sufficiently sharp
distinction between a **retained arithmetic anchor** and the temporary
ordinary source currently visited by the routing cursor.  That distinction
is essential.  In particular the canonical continuation

\[
O(x,e)\longrightarrow O(A(x),1-e)
\]

usually increases the displayed ordinary source.  It must therefore never
be counted as a numerical-source decrease, or even as an update of the
retained source anchor.

This section records only the normalizer theorem that is actually supplied
by Sections 91--135: it applies after an entry has already been supplied
with the source/token provenance required by those sections.

### Retained data versus routing data

A marked normalizer configuration has two kinds of data.

1. **Retained data.**  These are the arithmetic source anchor and the finite
   multiset of certified WIN/LOSS proof-height tokens whose provenance has
   been proved by the entry lemma or by a preceding strict transition.
2. **Routing data.**  These include the current obligation source, the
   current phase, a constant-tail or factor exponent, and the bounded
   A/B-control state.  Routing data may increase numerically.  They are not
   inserted into the retained rank merely because they are finite.

A token belongs to the retained multiset only when its outcome is known and
a comparison with the token that it replaces has actually been proved.
Likewise a numerical source is allowed to replace the retained source anchor
only when the cited lemma proves a strict comparison with that anchor.

Let \(M\) be the retained outer token multiset and let \(a\) be the retained
arithmetic anchor of the typed normalizer entry.  At a fixed pair \((a,M)\),
all temporary values such as \(x,A(x),B(A(x))\), signed quotients, and tail
exponents are routing data unless a cited transition explicitly promotes
one of them to a strict new anchor or a certified lower token.

### Why canonical A-growth is harmless

Section 93 treats a canonical A-continuation as one finite macro, rather
than as repeated changes of the retained anchor.  Starting at an
A-selecting obligation, at most four side tests occur before one of the
following happens:

- a genuinely strict retained-source exit;
- a certified boundary/token replacement;
- entry into the B-selecting phase;
- an exceptional adjacent frame over an actual lower LOSS token.

Thus the intermediate cursor values
\(A(x),A^2(x),\ldots\) are never compared lexicographically with the old
anchor.  In particular no inference of the form

\[
A(x)>x\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\text{the retained numerical rank changed}
\]

is used in either direction.

The B-selecting switch is likewise ranked only by statements actually
proved in Section 96.  A valuation at least three gives the stated strict
source alternative in the typed source/provenance context.  In the
valuation-two recycle, the first occurrence installs the finite WIN/LOSS
witness described there, and every further compatible recycle replaces
that witness by a certified lower descendant.  The witness, rather than
the possibly larger temporary cursor, is the well-founded quantity on that
branch.

### Factor and high-return routing

Sections 94--95 treat the exponent-one and higher factor forks.  Their
conclusions are again typed: a branch either performs a strict retained
source transition or carries an actual finite token whose later
replacement is certified.  Sections 97--103 attach every high return to its
incoming finite provenance before a new marked task is installed.
Sections 104--135 then transport that provenance through every tail length.
In particular Section 135 gives, for every marked tail of length at least
three, the strict token replacement

\[
\{h(q),h(y)\}\longmapsto\{h(q),h(r)\},
\qquad h(r)<h(y),
\]

before the inner factor scan is allowed to restart.  Sections 133--134
treat the short terminal rows without inserting an unrelated endpoint
height into the retained multiset.

Consequently the equal-retained-data routing relation generated by
Sections 91--135 has no operation that silently reinitializes either the
retained source anchor or the retained token multiset.  Canonical A-streaks
are finite, valuation-two recycling is controlled by its installed finite
witness, and every high-return cross-transition is strict before a fresh
marked task is installed.

We therefore retain the following theorem, with its scope stated
explicitly:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
For every **typed entry** whose source and proof-token provenance is one of
the entry types proved in Sections 91--135, the corresponding
marked/obligation routing relation is well-founded inside the fibre in
which those retained data are fixed.
\end{minipage}}
\]

The well-founded-fibre lemma of Section 132 then permits a strict retained
source transition to reset later data and permits a certified token
replacement, at fixed retained source, to reset only the routing data that
come after that token comparison.

This theorem deliberately does **not** assert that an arbitrary
factorful exponent-one state has already acquired such a typed entry.  That
was the missing upstream claim in the previous Section 137 and is treated
next.

## 137. Corrected entry audit and the arbitrary factorful exponent-one family

The previous version of this section contained the assertion that the
common child of the exponent-one/two boundary has a strictly smaller
**coefficient source**.  Section 15 proves only that its canonical odd
coefficient is smaller.  Those assertions are not equivalent in the
presence of powers of three.

For example, take

\[
s=1,\qquad J(1)=5,\qquad a=3J(1)=15,\qquad \epsilon=1.
\]

The common child is

\[
p=R(3a-\epsilon)=R(44)=22.
\]

Its canonical coefficient is \(11<15\), but

\[
11=J(3),
\]

so its coefficient source is \(3>1\).  The old source-descent sentence is
therefore false and is withdrawn.

The second defect in the old entry audit was the use of Section 16 as if it
removed powers of three from exponent one.  It does not: its reverse-frame
lemma starts at exponent at least two.  Thus the family

\[
\boxed{Q_1^\epsilon(3^kJ(s)),\qquad k>0}
\]

requires its own entry normalization.

### Universal two-level normalization

The arithmetic part of that normalization is already available in Sections
79--81 and is completely general.

Let

\[
a=J(s),\qquad k\ge1,
\]

and suppose

\[
R_k:=Q_1^\epsilon(3^ka)
\]

is DRAW.  Put

\[
P_k:=Q_2^\epsilon(3^ka),
\qquad
C_i:=B(P_i)=B(R_i),
\qquad
T_i:=A(R_i).
\]

Since \(R_k\) is DRAW, the adjacent frame \(\{P_k,R_k\}\) contains a
DRAW.  Section 80 therefore gives

\[
\boxed{\{C_k,T_k,C_{k+1},T_{k+1}\}\text{ contains a DRAW}.}
\]

Choose a level \(i\in\{k,k+1\}\) at which such a DRAW exit occurs and
factor its signed numerator as

\[
3^{i+1}J(s)+1-2\epsilon=2^vJ(t),
\qquad v\ge1.
\]

Then

\[
T_i=Q_v^{1-\epsilon}(J(t)).
\]

If \(v\ge2\), Section 81 gives the exact raw sibling

\[
\boxed{C_i=Q_{v-1}^{1-\epsilon}(J(t)).}
\]

Thus every DRAW on either of these two exits lies in an explicitly typed
factor-free lift or adjacent factor-free frame over the returned source
\(t\).

If \(v=1\), the signed exit is already the factor-free exponent-one lift

\[
T_i=Q_1^{1-\epsilon}(J(t)).
\]

If instead the raw exit \(C_i\) is DRAW, Section 81 proves the genuine
strict comparison

\[
\boxed{\rho(C_i)<t.}
\]

No comparison between \(t\) and the old source \(s\) is asserted here.
That omission is essential: in general \(t\) can be larger than \(s\).

We have therefore proved the following replacement for the false four-step
normalization used by the old Section 137:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
Every factorful exponent-one DRAW is normalized in at most two consecutive
factor levels to a factor-free signed lift, a factor-free adjacent frame,
or a valuation-one raw DRAW carrying a strict descent relative to its
returned source.  The normalization is exact and uses no bound on the
factor exponent.
\end{minipage}}
\]

This closes the omitted **arithmetic case split** identified in the
external audit.  It does not, by itself, prove the global rank statement.

### The remaining attachment obligation

To enter Section 136, one must still attach the returned lift/frame to the
retained outer source/token data without increasing that retained
projection.  It is not legitimate simply to replace the old anchor \(s\)
by the returned source \(t\), because \(t>s\) can occur.  Nor is it
legitimate to appeal to Sections 87--89 without checking their hypotheses:
Section 89's hidden first-factor parent uses the additional congruence of
the typed frames constructed there and is not a lemma about an arbitrary
initial exponent-one factorful DRAW.

For clarity, the exact missing statement is now isolated as follows.

**Arbitrary exponent-one attachment lemma (remaining).**  Assume that
\(s\) is the globally least coefficient source of a DRAW and that

\[
Q_1^\epsilon(3^kJ(s))\text{ is DRAW},\qquad k>0.
\]

Starting from the universal two-level normalization above, prove that one
can choose an outcome-compatible DRAW continuation for which, after
finitely many typed moves, at least one of the following holds:

1. an actual DRAW has retained coefficient source strictly below \(s\);
2. an already carried finite proof token is replaced by certified lower
   descendant token(s), with retained source \(s\) unchanged;
3. the DRAW enters one of the typed Section 136 normalizer states while the
   same retained outer source/token data are preserved.

The returned arithmetic source \(t\) may be stored as routing data in the
third alternative, but it may not replace the retained source \(s\) merely
because it is the current cursor.

There is useful additional structure for proving this lemma.  If \(s>0\),
the ordinary state \(s\) itself cannot be DRAW: its own coefficient source
satisfies

\[
\rho(s)\le\frac{s-1}{6}<s,
\]

contradicting the defining minimality of \(s\).  Hence \(s\) is WIN or
LOSS and supplies genuine finite proof-tree data.  The universal source
trichotomy of Section 54 and the provenance lemmas of Sections 57 and
61--64 are therefore the natural tools for transporting that finite datum
through the first factor exit.  The case \(s=0\) has the separate exact
zero-source normalization of Sections 71--73.

What has **not** yet been proved is that those ingredients cover every
outcome orientation of the arbitrary first entry without an unranked reset.
Until that final attachment lemma is supplied, the entry trichotomy required
by Section 132 is incomplete.

Accordingly the old boxed assertion

\[
\text{``Every such continuation lowers the projection or enters Section
136.''}
\]

is withdrawn at this point.  The correct status is: the arithmetic
exhaustiveness defect is repaired, while the rank/provenance attachment of
the arbitrary exponent-one family remains open.

## 138. Global theorem status after the corrected entry audit

Sections 129 and 132 remain valid order-theoretic statements, and Section
136 remains a well-founded normalizer theorem for entries whose retained
source/token provenance has actually been established.  The height-one
normalization of Sections 69--90 also remains available with the hypotheses
stated in those sections.

However the global no-DRAW conclusion requires the entry trichotomy to be
complete for **every** DRAW of globally least coefficient source.  Section
137 now shows explicitly that the arbitrary family

\[
Q_1^\epsilon(3^kJ(s)),\qquad k>0,
\]

has a complete two-level arithmetic normalization, but the returned source
can increase and the required attachment lemma has not yet been proved.
Therefore the previous proof of the global theorem cannot presently be
completed.

The correct implication is conditional:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{minipage}{0.88\linewidth}
If the arbitrary exponent-one attachment lemma of Section 137 is proved,
then every minimum-source and height-one entry has exactly the three
ranked outputs required by Sections 129, 132, and 136.  The marked
well-foundedness argument then excludes an infinite DRAW macro-path and the
no-DRAW theorem follows exactly as in the previous final assembly.
\end{minipage}}
\]

Until that attachment lemma is proved, the theorem

\[
\text{``every odd positive start reaches }1\text{ under optimal play''}
\]

must be recorded as **OPEN IN THIS MANUSCRIPT** rather than proved.

This status change concerns the global assembly, not the already proved
local arithmetic identities.  In particular the conjugated normal form,
strict contraction of \(B\), finite proof-height formalism, multiset rank,
universal factor-level coupling, and the exact two-level normalization
above remain valid with their stated hypotheses.
