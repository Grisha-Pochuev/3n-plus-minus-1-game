## New reduction obtained today

The exact `D=2` phase is always B-selecting.  For example:

```text

t=1, g=1: y=202, w=Q_2^0(J(y))  (B-selecting)
```

Because `m>=2`, the two low bits of `w` equal `delta`, so phase `delta`
selects `B(w)` and `B(w)<w`.  This closes the subcase in which the original
three/two frame enters its two/one factor pair: all four exits lower the
active source from `w` (directly or after the standard B-transfer).  It does
not close the initial common-side exit `w DRAW`; the coefficient transition
at `J(w)` is not a move from `w`.  That direct-DRAW lift is the remaining
unbounded branch.  The diagnostic is `scripts/inspect_d2_rows.py`.

The direct branch nevertheless always exposes a typed boundary: if `B(w)` is
DRAW then `B(w)<w` is a strict numerical exit; otherwise `B(w)` is WIN and
`w -> B(w)` is a DRAW-to-WIN boundary while the DRAW continues through
`A(w)`.  This strongly suggests that the orange and red items are one
remaining lifecycle problem: retain this exact boundary witness through the
generic normalizer until the next high return, then prove a descendant
replacement or a smaller-source exit.  That carry theorem is still open.
